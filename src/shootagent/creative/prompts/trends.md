# What performs now: formats, platform signals, hook rules

October 2026 research. Borrow mechanisms, never examples: each example is a different home on purpose.
Rebuild the device from this shoot's photos and facts.

## Formats and devices that perform

1. **The home is the premise.** Cast the listing in a format everyone knows (dating show, nature
   documentary, heist); one real feature carries the joke. Ex: a ranch "enters the villa" as a contestant.
   Why: the best agent skits make the property itself the premise (The Close, Business Insider, Sep 2026).
   Twist: the genre the photos justify, one genre beat per shot.
2. **The honest roast.** Name the limitation first, deadpan, then pay it off with a real feature. Ex: "The
   kitchen is also the hallway. It's a great hallway." Why: candour reads as trust and humour gets sent;
   Business Insider (Sep 2026) profiles an agent who roasts the homes she sells. Twist: an object delivers the
   roast; every jab true.
3. **Find-it game.** Say what to find in second one, scatter it across fast shots, confirm with a punch_in or
   on the loop. Ex: "One drawer in this kitchen is not a drawer." Why: hunting earns rewatches and answer
   comments; an agent's first hide-and-seek listing video reached about 14 million views (NAR, Apr 2026).
   Twist: hide a selling feature, not a gimmick.
4. **Speed tour.** The whole home at a sprint: 0.6-0.8 s cuts, whips, punch_ins, a running count, then one
   long hold. Ex: a colonial in nine seconds, then four on the window seat. Why: high-speed tours built one
   agent a following of over 2 million (Business Insider, Sep 2026). Twist: ramp to a dead stop on one
   detail, or run it backwards.
5. **Price as plot.** The price is a climax, puzzle or punchline, never a label. Ex: three wrong guesses
   type into a chat bubble, then the price_tag lands. Why: anyone can judge a price; agent guides call it
   the surest comment driver (Blitzcut, 2026, vendor blog). Twist: tie the number to something visible,
   use only listed facts, skip the genre's stock phrasing.
6. **Small-space breakdown.** A calm architect's tour of how a small plan works: zones, sightlines, visible
   storage. Ex: a studio as a list_reveal of four zones on one wall. Why: Never Too Small built millions of
   YouTube subscribers on small-space tours (Social Blade). Twist: one design decision per shot, never room
   names.
7. **Colour thread.** Follow one accent colour through the home and cut on it. Ex: a white condo where
   every room hides one mustard object. Why: interiors viewers know the "unexpected red theory" designer
   trend (Apartment Therapy, 2024). Twist: a colour the photos actually hold; test the theory, don't assert
   it.
8. **A day in the home.** Each photo is a time-stamped moment of one day. Ex: a loft: 7:10 keys on the hook,
   23:40 lamp off. Why: a Philadelphia agent stages "day in the life" showings so buyers feel how a home
   lives (Realtor.com, Aug 2026). Twist: measure time in cups of tea; claim no light the photos don't show.
9. **Commentary on the listing.** Annotate the photos like a sharp friend scrolling: stickers, asides,
   bland listing words corrected into what the photo proves. Ex: a sticker on a ranch's bonus room: "a
   hallway with ambition". Why: Zillow Gone Wild grew from listing commentary on Instagram into an
   HGTV series (NPR, 2024). Twist: the corrections set the edit's rhythm.
10. **Quiet, sound-led tour.** No music bed; one deliberate sound per beat, whisper-paced typewriter text.
   Ex: a cabin, one click as each lamp appears, silence on the view. Why: quiet interrupts a loud feed;
   whispering became one viral agent's signature (NAR, Apr 2026). Twist: the sounds count something; the
   text still works on mute.
11. **Told as a chat thread.** A DM exchange in typing_chat cards, photos as attachments. Ex: "wait, why
   does the bathroom have a window seat", then the photo. Why: Instagram weights sends for reaching
   non-followers (below); a reel shaped like a DM begs forwarding. Twist: unexpected correspondents, no
   invented facts.
12. **The seamless loop.** The last line completes the first; cut back to the opening frame. Ex: "...which
   is why the door is green" loops into "Nobody asks why the door is green." Why: replays add to watch time
   (below). Twist: plant a second detail only the rewatch reveals.

## What the platforms reward, as publicly reported

Instagram
- Platform statement (Instagram Ranking Explained, Adam Mosseri, May 2023): Reels rank mostly on predicted
  reshares, watching to the end, likes and audio-page visits, plus surveys of whether a reel was worth your
  time; low-resolution, watermarked, muted, bordered or reposted reels are shown less.
- Mosseri, January 2025, reported by Social Media Today: the top three signals are watch time, likes and
  sends; likes count slightly more with followers, sends slightly more with non-followers.
- Saves and comments are in the activity the 2023 explainer says Instagram reads, but not in the 2025 top
  three.
- Loops: no platform statement found calls loops a signal, but Meta's Instagram API docs count replays in a
  reel's total watch time.
- Instagram's blog via TechCrunch, April 30, 2026: accounts that regularly repost others' work stop being
  recommended; it says it rewards original or transformed work.
- Instagram's @creators account via Business Today, December 2025: at most five hashtags per post.
- Mosseri's year-end post via afaqs, Dec 31, 2025: "imperfection becomes a signal" as AI makes polish cheap.
  A forecast, not a ranking rule.

TikTok
- Platform statement (How TikTok recommends videos, Newsroom, 2020, still its published explainer): likes,
  shares, follows and comments; captions, sounds and hashtags; finishing a longer video is a strong signal;
  follower count and past hits are not direct factors.
- Platform guidance for ads, not organic ranking (TikTok Ads Help, creative best practices): hook within 6
  seconds through suspense or surprise, the proposition within 3, use sound, shoot 9:16.
- MacRumors, November 2025: viewers can dial AI-generated content down in For You; TikTok labels AI
  content. Stay photo-true.
- Length targets and "first hour" rules come from marketing blogs, not platforms.

## Ten hook rules

1. Frame one is the most specific image in the shoot, never a wide establishing shot, logo or name card
   (Blitzcut and CloudPano, 2026, vendor blogs).
2. The on-screen line works on mute and reads in a second. Clarity beats cleverness: say plainly what the
   viewer gets (BAM, Jul 2026).
3. Premise by second 3, tension by second 6 (TikTok Ads Help).
4. Stack hooks: picture, text and voice each add a different piece (BAM, 2025).
5. Start mid-thought or mid-action: a pattern break (BAM, 2024).
6. One checkable specific: a listed number or a named object in the photos. Swap test: if the line could
   caption another listing, rewrite it.
7. A hook is a promise. Pay it off on screen, ideally on the loop point.
8. A number on screen must count, compare, contradict or resolve something.
9. Write for a send: aim at a situation one friend is in, never at a type of person.
10. Give sound a job on frame one (a hit, a click, a first word); the hook stays complete on mute.
